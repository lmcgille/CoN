The Hollow Reach

When the gate network failed, forty systems were sealed inside a dust-choked cluster with no way home and no one to call. Two hundred years later, the habitats that survived have stopped waiting for rescue. They have started counting each other's ice.

40 systems · 9 great habitats 8 hit classes 37 units Legions of 9 · wings of 6 · squadrons of 3 Volley cycles every hour

The world

A closed cluster, slow lanes between stars, and the one resource everybody drinks, breathes and burns.

The Severance happened in a single afternoon: every gate in the Reach went dark at once, and the forty systems inside became an island. The great habitats — rotating cylinders, hollowed asteroids, domed moons — learned to live on what was inside the dust. Two centuries later they are nations in everything but name.

Ships move between systems along lanes: slow, predictable gravitational corridors. Each system holds one or more habitats (the cities of this world) and a scatter of outer bodies — moons, rock fields, ice belts — that function as countryside. The spaces between systems are not empty: the Reach is full of nebula, thick ionised dust where sensors fail and cloaked ships hide.

Victory is Dominion: a daily tally of the habitats, bodies and the three Relics — pre-Severance gate fragments at Ossuary, Tamsin's Rest and the Hollow Gate itself — that a power (or a pact) controls.

Places that decide wars

Habitat interiors

Cramped, pressurised corridors. Heavy walkers fight at a crawl and a fraction of their power; dug-in legions are nearly impossible to remove.

The Dust Plains

The terraformed flats of Corvin IV and a few other worlds. Open ground where walker columns are at their best.

Ice moons

Cold, broken terrain. Cryo-Rangers excel; most units fight and move badly.

Clutter fields

Planetary rings and asteroid belts. Ringrunner craft hide inside the clutter below long-range sensor lock — only close-in flak can find them.

Gravity wells

The space close around planets and habitats. Cutterskiffs thrive; cloaked Shroud hulls are sluggish, half-shielded and easy to find.

Deep nebula

The dark between systems. Shroud hulls are lethal here; small ships lose shielding and stability in the ion storms.

Holdings

Home habitats

Full output, can raise anything. The seat-habitat raises Allegiance across its own system.

Seized habitats

A quarter output, very low starting Allegiance. These secede.

Bonded habitats

Seized habitats you have bound with a Covenant (costs Crew). Half output, can raise units.

Outer bodies

Moons, belts and rocks. Little output unless you build a Rig on a seam. Never secede.

Allegiance and secession

Each holding has Allegiance 0–100, recalculated once per standard day toward a target. Distance (in lane-hours) from your seat, the number of active wars, enemy forces in-system, shortages of Ice or Writ, bombardment casualties and enemy agents all drag the target down; structures and a calm neighbourhood push it up.

A seized habitat under about a third Allegiance may secede. A newly taken habitat starts at a quarter. If it secedes with no legion aboard, it simply becomes part of the Unmoored — a loose free-habitat movement that arms its own militias and does not stay put. A single legion on the habitat's Core usually crushes the uprising.

Rules of battle

The physics of war in the Reach.

Volley cycles

The first volley fires the moment forces meet; another every hour after. Each volley applies the attacker's rating for the defender's hit class. Mass drivers aimed at strikecraft do nothing; flak aimed at walkers does nothing.

The Core

Every habitat and body has a Core — the reactor, the hub, the docking spine. Forces holding the Core are dug in: they take heavily reduced damage and do not fire back. Forces near it but not on it trade volleys every cycle.

Formation limits

Ground legions fight at full strength to 9 units, strikecraft wings to 6, capital squadrons to 3. Beyond that, each addition is worth less, then nothing, then negative — and the whole formation moves at its slowest member.

Shield, Core-line and Wake

Damage on a formation is split by position. Shield units soak the most, Core-line units some, Wake units almost none. Position is fixed by unit type.

Stand-off fire

Mass drivers, rail mortars and broadside cruisers strike from range. Something that can't reach back can't fire back. Volley, withdraw, wait out the cycle, volley again.

Only legions seize

A holding changes hands when a seizing unit reaches its Core. Walkers, hulls, strikecraft, Shade Cells and Freeblades cannot seize anything.

Open void and clutter

Strikecraft in the open void are tracked by long-range lance batteries. Ringrunners hide in rings, belts and upper atmosphere, below lance-lock entirely. Only close-in flak — spindles, tenders, interceptors — touches them.

Flak inside vs flak nearby

Flak within the targeted formation fires on every attacker and every torpedo, with no recharge. Flak merely covering a nearby formation fires, then recharges — saturate it and the rest get through.

Sensors and cloaks

Sight shows contacts; unscanned formations appear as unresolved returns. Sensor spires and Lantern ships read drive signatures at range. Cloaked units are invisible until they fire or a matching scanner (ground, void or shroud) comes close.

Wells and nebula

Near a planet is a gravity well; between systems is deep nebula. Cutterskiffs love wells and suffer in nebula. Shroud hulls are the opposite — sluggish and half-shielded in wells, deadly in nebula.

Barges

Legions travelling between bodies ride barges, boarded at a Slipway. Barges have no weapons and thin hulls; boarding and landing leave a legion helpless for hours. Void Marines can board any station or body directly.

Launch cradles

Strikecraft fly sorties from a home cradle and return to rearm. Docked craft are helpless. Wreck the cradle and everything docked there is stranded until it is rebuilt.

Hit classes

Which of the attacker's ratings applies depends on what the target is hit as.

The three compacts

Every power follows one of three engineering traditions. Roles are identical; toughness, price and research timing differ.

The Ardent

Chronicle: The Ice War

The thirty-day war between the Ardent Host and the Lumen–Freehold pact, as reconstructed from both sides' logs.

Cycle 1–3Forges and walkers

Warlord Hesper Vane had been waiting for this war for nine years and had the walkers to prove it. On the first standard day the Ardent Host lit its forges; on the second, the Kesh yards rolled out Juggernauts — three-legged siege walkers, fifty metres tall, with armour plates you could park a shuttle on. By the third, two full legions of them were loaded on barges and pointed at Corvin IV, whose mining collective owned the richest Null-salt seams in the Reach and the poorest army.

The Lumen Accord built three forges, an archive, and a launch cradle. Chancellor Idris Oyelaran's admirals asked when they would get ships. "When the forges have paid for them," she said, and went back to the tariff tables.

At Tessaly, Commodore Sabine Achterberg commissioned a Corsair destroyer and an Aegis tender and parked them inside her own gravity well. The tender was a fat, ugly ship with almost no guns, a shipyard's worth of flak turrets, and nothing else to recommend it.

Cycle 4–8The Dust Plains

Corvin IV's Dust Plains were made for Juggernauts: flat, firm, open to the horizon. The Collective's wardens stood in the open and were walked over. Vane took four habitats and the seams in five days, and broadcast each Core falling to every system in the Reach.

Each time a Core fell, the Juggernauts moved on. Legions were slow; walkers were glorious. No one was left aboard any of the four habitats except the people who lived there.

Cycle 9Out of the rings

Corvin IV has rings — wide, dense, and full of ice and rock. Oyelaran's first combat wing was six Wasp ringrunners: small, mean, anti-armour craft that fly inside clutter, where long-range lance batteries cannot get a lock.

Vane's lead legion — four Juggernauts, two mass drivers, nothing else — was halted on the Plains below the ring plane. Two Ardent lance batteries a few kilometres back watched the sky. They saw a perfectly clear sky: the Wasps were down in the ring shadow, then down in the dust, under the lance horizon the entire time.

The first volley gutted two Juggernauts. Juggernauts carry no flak — their guns cannot elevate — so the Wasps took their time. The next cycle, another volley. By the time the legion's commander understood what was happening, she was the only one left to understand it.

"Lance batteries report no contacts. Repeat, no contacts." — last logged transmission from Lance Battery Kesh-14, Cycle 9

Cycle 10–12More lances

Vane had a fix ready: more lance batteries, and a Bastion array — the most expensive anti-ship and anti-torpedo platform in the Ardent catalogue. None of them could see a ringrunner. The Wasps kept coming. A single Flak Spindle inside each legion, at a fraction of the price, would have ended the problem in a cycle. Ardent doctrine called spindles "defensive."

On Cycle 11, the Freeholds of Tessaly and the Lumen Accord signed a Dominion pact: their holdings would count as one.

Cycle 13The tender

Vane had one good strike wing: six Hammerhead bombers, borrowed technology, the pride of the Host. Her scouts found the Freehold squadron anchored in the Tessaly well: a Corsair, a Broadside cruiser — the single most dangerous ship in the region, and notoriously vulnerable to strikecraft — and the fat Aegis tender. It looked like the chance of the war.

The tender was inside the squadron. Its turrets fired on every bomber, every volley, without pausing to recharge. The first volley came before the Hammerheads reached release range. Two survived it; one made it home.

"I didn't lose to the cruiser. I lost to the ship I didn't think was a ship." — Warlord Vane, to her war council

Cycle 14–16Secession

The four Corvin habitats had been sitting at a quarter Allegiance since the day they fell, far from Kesh, full of miners who remembered being free. On Cycle 14 two of them seceded in the same hour. There were no legions aboard. The Unmoored had their banners up before the Ardent garrison commander received the alert — because there was no Ardent garrison commander. By Cycle 16 a rebel militia was raiding the barge lane that fed Vane's front.

Cycle 17–20The long siege of Lucent

Vane gathered everything into one immense legion — nineteen walkers, drivers and infantry — and marched it on the Lumen fortress-habitat of Lucent. Past nine units every addition made it fight worse; at nineteen it was a lumbering crowd fighting at two-thirds strength and moving at the speed of its slowest driver.

Oyelaran put Breaker Lances and Line Legions on Lucent's Core, where they dug in and simply absorbed. Two moons out she placed three mass driver batteries. Each hour the drivers slid into range, fired one volley into the column, and slid back out before the column could close. They never took a shot in return. When the Host finally reached Lucent it was bloodied and slow, and it threw itself at defenders who were taking a fraction of its damage and giving none of it back.

Cycle 22The nebula crossing

Vane's last reserve, two legions on barges, went out across the Orrin Nebula to land behind Tessaly. The Ardent's destroyers were still on the slips, so the barges went alone. In the nebula, two Freehold Shroud Hunters had been drifting for nine days with their cloaks up. Barges cannot shoot back. The barges never reached the far side.

Cycle 23–28Stranded

The pact's last blow was a single night of seeker torpedoes on the two Ardent launch cradles. Everything Vane had rebuilt since Cycle 13 was docked there. With her strikecraft stranded and her lance batteries still blind to anything in the clutter, the Host's remaining legions were simply hunted. On Cycle 28 the pact's Dominion passed the Host's, and the war ended at the next count.

Why the Host fell

  1. Walkers without flak. Juggernauts cannot fire on ringrunners. A Flak Spindle in each legion was the whole answer.
  2. Long-range defence against a short-range threat. Lance batteries and Bastion arrays are blind to clutter. She bought the wrong shield, twice.
  3. Bombers into a tender. Flak inside a squadron fires on every attacker, every volley. The least impressive hull was the deadliest.
  4. No legions aboard. Seized habitats at a quarter Allegiance, left empty, seceded and attacked her rear.
  5. The nineteen-walker legion. Overloaded, slow, kited by drivers it could never catch.
  6. Storming a held Core. Defenders who don't fire back also barely bleed.
  7. Unescorted barges in the nebula. Shroud hulls are built for exactly that.
  8. Craft in the cradle. A docked wing is a target list.
