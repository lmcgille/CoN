World VI · Undersea

Pelagia

After the Scald made the surface unliveable, humanity went down. Two centuries later, the seafloor holds forty pressurised cities, and none of them has ever seen the sun. They fight in three dimensions, in the dark, by sound.

The world

The war happens in layers. Learn the layers and you have learned the war.

The surface of Pelagia is a scalded, storm-wracked desert of water. Nobody goes there. Below it lies a continental shelf at a hundred to three hundred metres, studded with domed cities and kelp farms, and beyond the shelf edge the Abyss: black, crushing, enormous. The cities are linked by pipelines and by the routes their craft fly through open water.

A Pelagian war is fought in four layers:

MIDWATER
Open water above the reefs. Fast gliders fly here — strike craft that sonar arrays can track at long range.
REEFLINE
Just above the seafloor, among kelp and coral. Reef-skimmers hug it, hidden in the sonar clutter of the bottom. Long-range arrays cannot hear them.
SEAFLOOR
Where the cities stand. Walkers, crawlers and suited divers fight on the bottom. Only divers can take a city.
THERMOCLINE & ABYSS
Big hulls cruise the thermocline lanes. Below it, in the Abyss, silent hulls hide where no sound carries.

Victory is Ascendance: a tally, every 24 hours, of cities, seafloor tracts, and the three Vents — the great hydrothermal fields of Black Smoker, Ember Rift and the Cathedral — whose heat powers everything.

Places that decide wars

The Sand Flats

Open shelf. Leviathan walkers at full strength; divers in the open are exposed.

The Domes

City interiors: corridors, locks, bulkheads. Walkers are useless inside; dug-in divers hold for days.

The Seamounts

Underwater mountains. Ridge Divers thrive; everything else struggles.

The Kelp Forests

Dense and tangling. Slow for all, deadly for big machines.

Shelf water

Shallow (by Pelagian standards). Cutters are nimble; silent hulls are cramped and easy to hear.

The Abyss

Below the shelf edge. Silent hulls are lethal; small craft are crushed.

Holdings

Home domes

Full output. The capital dome steadies Calm in its region.

Seized domes

A quarter output; Calm near zero. These breach.

Compacted domes

Seized domes integrated by Compact (costs Divers). Half output; can raise units.

Seafloor tracts

The floor between cities. Little output unless you build a Harvester. Tracts never breach.

Calm and the Breach

Every dome and tract has Calm. It settles each cycle toward a target set by distance from the capital, the number of wars, enemy units near, shortages of Protein or Credit, casualties from bombardment, enemy agents, and the structures you've built.

A seized dome below a third Calm may breach — its people seal the locks and declare for the Drift, a free-city movement that arms its own militia. Newly seized domes start at a quarter. Once a day, at an unpredictable hour, the dome either holds or breaches. If none of your divers are inside, it simply goes. One squad on the dome's Keel-lock usually reopens it.

Rules of battle

Ping cycles

The first exchange happens on contact; another every ping cycle (45 minutes). Each weapon's rating applies against the target's hit class: an anti-walker spike gun does little to a diver.

The Keel-lock

Every dome and tract has a Keel-lock. Units holding it are sealed in: they take much less damage and do not fire back. Units outside it, not in it, trade fire every cycle.

Pods, schools, wolfpacks

A seafloor pod fights at full strength up to 11 units, a craft school up to 8, a hull wolfpack up to 3. Beyond that, each addition is worth less, then nothing, then negative — and a formation moves at its slowest unit's speed.

Lead, mid, trail

Fire on a formation falls mostly on the lead, less on the mid, least on the trail. Unit type decides.

Stand-off

Pressure mortars, torpedo racks and heavy hulls fire from range. Targets that can't reach back can't fire back. Fire, withdraw, wait out the cycle, fire again.

Only divers seize

A dome or tract changes hands only when seizing divers reach its Keel-lock. Walkers, craft, hulls, ghost teams and contract crews cannot seize.

Midwater and reefline

Gliders in midwater are tracked by long-range sonar arrays. Reef-skimmers fly in the reefline, inside the bottom's echo-clutter — invisible to arrays. Only flechette turrets, picket hulls and interceptors can hear and hit them.

In the formation vs near it

Flechette turrets in the targeted formation fire on every attacker and torpedo, every cycle, without reloading. Turrets merely near it fire, then reload — so saturate them.

Sonar and silence

Close contacts are heard; distant ones are only "noise" until a scout identifies them. Sonar arrays and Listener craft hear machinery at long range. Silent units — ghost teams, silent gliders, abyssal hulls — are inaudible until they fire or a matching listener (floor, water or deep) closes in.

Shelf and Abyss

Cutters love shelf water and are crushed in the Abyss. Silent hulls are the reverse — cramped and loud on the shelf, deadly in the dark.

Carriers

Divers moving between distant cities ride carrier pods boarded at a hull yard. Carriers are unarmed and thin-skinned; boarding and unloading leave divers helpless for hours. Breacher Divers and Crab Walkers can cross open water on their own.

The dock

Craft sortie from a home dock and return to recharge. Docked craft are helpless. Breach the dock and every craft there is stranded.

Logs: The Heat War

Reconstructed from recovered command logs, sonar transcripts and the personal recorder of Sonar Technician First Class Ines Morrow, Lanternfall Consortium.

Cycle 1–40Leviathans

The Trench Dominion had been building towards this for years. On the first day of the war Prefect Rhoswen Kaal opened two new foundry rings; on the second, the Marrow walker bays flooded and released Leviathans — six-legged seafloor walkers the size of a pressure dome, armoured against anything that swims. By the fourth day two pods of them were marching across the Sand Flats toward Black Smoker vent and the Fathom Cooperative that owned it.

Lanternfall built foundries, a lab ring and a glider dock, and nothing that could fight. Director Ferreira-Hale's staff asked for walkers. She told them the Air budget wouldn't stand it yet.

On the shelf, the Free Cities commissioned a hunter hull and an odd, slab-sided picket the shipwrights called a needle-hull — no heavy torpedoes at all, just a skin of flechette turrets — and parked both in shelf water outside their capital.

Cycle 41–150The Flats

The Sand Flats are open ground, and Leviathans on open ground are unstoppable. The Cooperative's divers were caught in the open and swept away. In five days Kaal held four Fathom domes and the vent. Each Keel-lock was opened, a Dominion code was uploaded, and the Leviathans marched on. No divers stayed behind.

MARROW COMMAND // 0611 — Fathom-3 seized. Pod advancing on Fathom-4. Garrison: none assigned. Reason: tempo.

Cycle 151From the reef

Lanternfall's first combat school was eight eel-skimmers: small, fast reefline craft carrying shaped charges, designed for one thing.

MORROW, PERSONAL RECORDER — We came in along the Kelp Line at two metres off the bottom. The Dominion had two sonar arrays covering the pod. I could hear them pinging the midwater — big, slow, sweeping pings, looking for gliders. We were under all of it. In the reef clutter we sounded like rocks.

The Dominion pod — four Leviathans, two mortar crawlers, nothing else — was halted at a pipeline junction. A Leviathan's weapons are built to fight along the floor; they cannot elevate onto a craft passing a few metres above its own back. The eel-skimmers made their first pass and two Leviathans went dark. Every cycle they made another pass. The arrays reported clear water the entire time.

MARROW ARRAY 7 // 1340 — No midwater contacts. No midwater contacts. Pod Kessel reports explosions. Unable to classify. No midwater contacts.

Cycle 160–220Louder arrays

Kaal ordered seven more sonar arrays and a Bastion interception grid for Marrow itself — the most expensive defence the Benthic lineage builds. None of them can hear the reefline. The eels kept coming. One flechette turret per pod, a cheap thing that hears the reef and shreds anything in it, would have ended it on the first pass. Benthic doctrine lists flechette turrets as "garrison equipment."

On cycle 200, the Free Cities and Lanternfall signed the Shelf Accord. Their Ascendance would be counted together.

Cycle 260The needle-hull

The Dominion did have strike craft — eight Manta strikers, bought from the Abyssal lineage before the war. Dominion intelligence found the Free Cities wolfpack in shelf water: the hunter hull, a Dreadnought hull (the most dangerous warship on the shelf, and famously soft against craft), and the slab-sided needle-hull, logged as "auxiliary."

FREE CITIES WOLFPACK ORCA // 0302 — Eight contacts, midwater, closing on Dreadnought. Needle engaging. Needle engaging. Needle engaging. Six down. Seven. Contact eight withdrawing.

The needle-hull was in the wolfpack. Its turrets fired on every Manta, every cycle, without reloading. Two Mantas reached release range. One got home.

Cycle 300–330Breach

Two of the four Fathom domes breached on the same night. They had sat at a quarter Calm since they fell — far from Marrow, short of Air, and with no Dominion divers inside. The locks sealed, the Drift's symbol went up on the comm channels, and by cycle 330 a Drift militia was cutting the Dominion's pipeline to the front.

Cycle 340–420Lanterndeep

Kaal concentrated everything — twenty-three units of walkers, crawlers and divers in one pod — and marched on the Lanternfall fortress-dome of Lanterndeep. Past eleven units every addition made the pod fight worse; at twenty-three it moved at a crawl and fought at two-thirds strength.

Lanternfall put spike-lancers and divers on Lanterndeep's Keel-lock, where they sealed in and absorbed. Two tracts back, three torpedo-rack crawlers slid forward every cycle, fired a spread into the great pod from beyond its reach, and slid back before it could close. They never took a hit. The pod reached Lanterndeep bloodied and slow and threw itself at sealed defenders who barely bled.

Cycle 450The shelf edge

Kaal's last reserve — two pods in carrier pods — went out over the shelf edge, across the Abyss, to land behind the Free Cities. The Dominion's own hunter hulls weren't finished; the carriers went alone.

FREE CITIES SILENT HULL WRAITH // 0118 — Day nine on station. Carrier pod contacts, eight, unescorted, passing overhead. Engaging.

Carriers cannot fight. There were no survivors.

Cycle 470–560Stranded

Lanternfall ended it with a single night of supercavitating torpedoes on the two Dominion glider docks. Every craft Kaal had rebuilt since cycle 260 was docked there. With her craft stranded and her arrays still deaf to the reefline, her remaining pods were hunted across the Flats. At the next tally the Shelf Accord's Ascendance passed the Dominion's.

MORROW, PERSONAL RECORDER — It's quiet tonight. You can hear the vents again.

After-action: why Marrow lost

  1. Walkers with no flechette turrets. They cannot fire upward. One turret per pod was the whole answer.
  2. Midwater arrays against the reefline. She bought the loudest ears in the sea and pointed them at the wrong layer.
  3. Strikers into a needle-hull. Turrets in the wolfpack fire on every attacker. The dullest hull was the deadliest.
  4. Empty domes. A quarter Calm, no divers, and they breached behind her.
  5. The twenty-three-unit pod. Too large to fight, too slow to catch the racks.
  6. Assaulting a sealed Keel-lock. Defenders who don't fire back barely bleed.
  7. Carriers across the Abyss, unescorted. That is what silent hulls wait for.
  8. Craft in the dock. A docked school is a target list.
