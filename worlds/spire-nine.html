World VII · Cyberpunk

Spire Nine

When the city government of Spire Nine went bankrupt in 2131, the court sold it — block by block, to whoever would pay. Twenty million people woke up as assets on a balance sheet. Now the corporations that bought them are settling the rest of the map the old-fashioned way.

The world

A vertical city with its feet in the sea, fought over in four layers.

Spire Nine is two hundred square kilometres of arcology towers, elevated highways, and pre-collapse neighbourhoods jammed into a river delta. The sea came up a long time ago. The bottom four floors of the old city are underwater; the streets there are canals, and the subway beneath them is a drowned maze everyone calls the Sump.

The city's land is divided into blocks (dense, valuable — this world's cities) and districts (everything between). Each player is a corporation, or a holdout community, that owns a headquarters tower and a handful of blocks. Victory is Market Share: a quarterly-style valuation, run every 24 hours by the Receivership Court, of the blocks, districts and three Assets of Record — the Desal Stack, the Old Exchange datacentre, and the Orbital Elevator ground station — that a charter or a consortium controls.

Terrain

The Expressways

Elevated multi-lane highways. Wide and flat. Heavy frames run at full strength; infantry on the asphalt dies.

The Hives

Dense residential stacks. Hardframes can't fit; dug-in street crews hold for days.

The Heights

Rooftop districts and old cliffside slums. Spiders thrive; everything else climbs.

The Overgrowth

Abandoned parkland gone wild. Slow and deadly for vehicles.

The Canals

Shallow drowned streets. Skiffs rule; Sump-runners are cramped, noisy and exposed.

The Sump

The flooded metro tunnels. Deep, black, perfect for submersibles; skiffs that enter rarely come out.

Holdings

Owned blocks

Full output. HQ raises Compliance nearby.

Acquired blocks

Taken by force. A quarter output; Compliance near zero. These riot.

Integrated blocks

Acquired blocks put through Integration (costs Staff). Half output; can raise crews.

Districts

The sprawl between. Low output unless you build a plant. Districts don't riot.

Compliance and riots

Compliance settles each day toward a target set by distance from HQ, number of active wars, enemy assets nearby, shortages, collateral damage, enemy agitators and your amenities.

An acquired block below about a third Compliance may riot. Fresh acquisitions sit at a quarter. Once a day, at a random time, the block either holds or blows. If you have no crew there, it simply goes over to the Unplugged — an off-grid movement that arms itself and spreads. One crew on the block's Junction usually ends a riot.

Rules of engagement

Firefight cycles

First contact resolves immediately; another cycle every 20 minutes. Each weapon uses its rating against the target's hit class.

The Junction

Every block and district has a Junction. A detail holding it is dug in: it takes much less damage and doesn't shoot back. A detail outside it trades fire every cycle.

Details, wings, flotillas

Street details fight at full strength up to 13, air wings up to 4, canal flotillas up to 5. Past that, diminishing, then zero, then negative — and a detail moves at its slowest asset's speed.

Point, body, tail

Incoming fire lands mostly on the point, less on the body, least on the tail. Asset type decides.

Indirect fire

Mortar teams, missile trucks and gunboats fire from range. Targets that can't reach back can't hit back. Fire, pull back, wait out the cycle, repeat.

Tagging

A block or district changes hands only when a tagging crew reaches the Junction. Frames, aircraft, boats, ghosts and contractors can't tag.

Skyline and alley

Aerodynes fly the skyline, above the towers, where rooftop lance grids track them. Kites fly at alley level, between buildings, below every rooftop grid's line of sight. Only shredder turrets, picket boats and interceptors hit kites.

In the detail vs near it

Turrets in the targeted detail fire on every attacker and every munition with no reload. Turrets near it fire, then reload — so flood them.

Sensors and ghosts

Nearby contacts are visible; distant ones are "unknown signal" until a scout IDs them. Sensor masts and Overwatch aerodynes track emissions at range. Ghosted assets are invisible until they fire or a matching sensor gets close.

Canal and Sump

Skiffs dominate the shallow canals and die in the Sump. Sump-runners are the reverse.

Ferries

Crews crossing water ride ferries boarded at a boathouse. Ferries are unarmed; boarding and landing leave a crew helpless for a while. Canal Rats and Hover-Haulers cross on their own.

The pad

Aircraft and kites launch from a home pad and return to recharge. Parked assets are helpless. Hit the pad and everything on it is stuck.

Feed: The Desal War

As it scrolled past on the open feeds, with commentary from Juno "Static" Reyes, a courier who saw most of it from a moped.

Day 1–3Buy, build, wait

[d1 09:14] @KVIndustrial Proud to announce three new fab floors and the immediate deployment of our Rhino hardframe line. Building the future of Spire Nine. #KVforward

Kessler-Volk had Rhinos — four-metre armoured hardframes with a cannon where the right arm should be — walking out of the shop by the second morning. By the third, two full details of them were parked at the edge of Lowmarket, which sits on top of the Desal Stack, which makes all the drinking water in the south city. Everyone knew what that meant.

Halo Systems announced a fab floor, an R&D floor and a new pad, and nothing with a gun. Their stock dropped four percent. The Chair did not comment.

Tidewater put two boats in the canal outside their HQ: a hunter boat with a sonar dome, and an ugly flat thing with a dozen shredder turrets on it and nothing else. The dockworkers called it the Hedgehog.

Day 4–8The expressway

Lowmarket's people met the Rhinos on the Southern Expressway, which was the worst place they could have picked. Wide, flat, nowhere to hide. Kessler-Volk tagged four Lowmarket blocks in five days, including the Stack. And then they rolled on to the next one. Every time.

I delivered noodles to one of those blocks on day nine. There wasn't a single KV crew in the building. Just the new logo on the lobby screens, and a lot of very quiet people.

Day 9Under the grid

Halo's first combat assets were four hunter-kites — little tank-killer drones with shaped charges, the kind that fly at alley height and never go above the third floor. They came in through the market streets, under the awnings.

KV had two rooftop lance grids covering their lead detail on the expressway. Lance grids watch the skyline. They watched it for an hour and saw nothing — because there was nothing there. The kites were down in the alleys and then under the overpass, and a Rhino's cannon can't elevate past the horizontal.

[d9 13:02] @SouthSideScanner massive explosions on the southern expressway, KV frames burning, NO AIRCRAFT VISIBLE what is happening
[d9 13:22] @SouthSideScanner another one. still nothing in the sky. this is insane
[d9 15:40] @KVIndustrial We are aware of reports of an incident on the Southern Expressway. Our air defence systems detected no hostile aircraft.

Every twenty minutes, another pass. By evening the detail was a row of scrap.

Day 10–12More grid

Kessler-Volk's answer was eight more lance grids and an Aegis dome on their HQ tower, the most expensive point-defence system on the market. None of it can see alley level. The kites kept coming. A single shredder turret per detail — a cheap, ugly, short-range thing every courier knows the sound of — would have ended it on the first pass. KV's procurement people had cut shredders from the budget as "low-value."

[d11 18:00] @TidewaterHQ + @HaloSystems: we are pleased to announce a strategic partnership. Valuations to be reported jointly.

Day 13The Hedgehog

KV still had an air wing: four strike aerodynes. Their spotters found the Tidewater flotilla moored in the Grand Canal: the hunter boat, a big gunboat (scariest thing on the water, and everyone knows gunboats can't defend themselves from the air), and the flat ugly Hedgehog, which KV's analysts logged as a "utility barge."

The Hedgehog was in the flotilla. Every one of its turrets fired at every aerodyne that dived on any boat in the group, every cycle, no reload. It sounded like the sky tearing. Two aerodynes got to launch range. One got home.

[d13 03:41] @GrandCanalCam just watched three corp aircraft get shredded by a garbage barge??
[d13 03:44] @GrandCanalCam correction four

Day 14–16Riot

Two of the four Lowmarket blocks blew on the same night. They'd been at a quarter Compliance since KV took them — far from KV's tower, short on water (ironic), and not one KV crew inside. When the riots started there was nobody to stop them. By morning the Unplugged held both blocks, and by day sixteen they were jamming KV's supply road to the front.

Day 17–20The Lumen Tower siege

KV put everything they had left — twenty-seven assets — into one enormous detail and pointed it at Halo's Lumen Tower. Past thirteen, each new asset made the detail worse. At twenty-seven it moved like a traffic jam and fought at about two-thirds strength.

Halo put railgun teams and street crews on the tower's Junction, where they dug in and just absorbed. Two districts back they parked three missile trucks. Every twenty minutes the trucks pulled forward, fired a salvo into the KV column from out of its reach, and pulled back before it could close. They never took a hit. KV reached the tower bloodied and slow and threw itself at defenders who barely bled.

Day 21The Sump

For a second front, KV loaded two details onto ferries and sent them through the Sump tunnels to come up behind Tidewater's docks. KV's own hunter boats weren't built yet, so the ferries went alone. Two Tidewater Sump-runners had been sitting in the dark of the old Line 4 tunnel for nine days. Ferries can't fight back.

[d21 01:12] @SumpWatch big debris field coming up the Line 4 vents. lots of KV orange.

Day 22–26Grounded

Halo ended it with one night of smart munitions on KV's two pads. Everything KV had rebuilt since day thirteen was parked there. With no air and grids that still couldn't see the alleys, the last KV details were hunted along the expressways by kites. At the next valuation, the Halo–Tidewater partnership's Market Share passed KV's, and the Court closed the case.

I still deliver to Lowmarket. The water's back on.

Analyst note: why Kessler-Volk lost

  1. Hardframes with no shredders. They can't shoot up. One turret per detail was the answer.
  2. Skyline grids against alley-level kites. Expensive defence pointed at the wrong altitude.
  3. Strike wing into the Hedgehog. Turrets inside the flotilla fire on every attacker. The ugliest boat was the deadliest.
  4. Untended acquisitions. A quarter Compliance, no crews, two riots.
  5. The twenty-seven-asset detail. Too big to fight, too slow to catch the trucks.
  6. Assaulting a dug-in Junction. Defenders who don't shoot back barely bleed.
  7. Unescorted ferries through the Sump. That's what runners wait for.
  8. Aircraft parked on known pads. Parked is targeted.

Feed II: The Consortium War

Same city, one year later. Static is still on the moped.

SetupTwo consortia

KV got broken up by the Court after the Desal War, and everybody else picked sides. The Halo Consortium: Halo Systems and Saint Ambrose Hospital Trust. The Tidewater Consortium: Tidewater Holdings and the Brokers. Lowmarket, rebuilt and armed now, joined nobody. Consortium of two means double the Market Share to win. Everybody knows that. Not everybody thinks about it.

Day 6Already inside

[d6 12:00] @TheBrokers Effective immediately, the Brokers are in a state of hostility with Halo Systems.
[d6 12:01] @SouthSideScanner wait Broker crews have been sitting in the Halo Arcade Junction for like a week??
[d6 12:03] @SouthSideScanner lol it just flipped. no shots fired

The Brokers had an access agreement with Halo for years — their couriers ran Halo freight and their crews rode along. When the freight stopped at Halo Arcade, the crews waited on the Junction. Then Mister Glass declared war, and the block was his.

Days 7–12Saint Ambrose isn't picking up

Halo asked Saint Ambrose for interceptors over the Arcade. Nothing. The Trust's fabs kept running and its pads stayed full. But as long as Ambrose stayed in the Consortium, the bar stayed doubled — dead weight with a seat at the table.

Days 10–14Boxed in

Halo ran the missile-truck play again: pull in, fire, pull out. Tidewater's detail was bait. While the trucks pulled back, two little packs of Mantis APCs and scout bikes came at them from both ends of the street at once. A truck can run from one direction. Not two. I rode past the wrecks the next morning.

Days 15–17The fixer and the salvo

Tidewater had a fixer with root access inside Halo since day two. The fixer knew something Halo didn't post: their main pad sat under an Aegis dome, but its shredders had gone forward to Lumen Tower. On day seventeen two hunter boats fired twelve smart munitions in one salvo. The dome got the first, recycled, got another. The rest hit the pad.

Days 18–22Clear skies, burning docks

[d22 02:15] @TidewaterHQ Aegis firmware update complete.
[d23 02:16] @GrandCanalCam ok now we can see them. ghost AVs. they were ghost AVs

Days 20–24The canal mouth

Two Halo hunter boats mined the Brokers' canal mouth one night. The Brokers' skiffs logged "faint sonar, probably trash." Three skiffs and two ferries later, the Brokers closed their own canal.

Day 25Blackout

Halo had the Arcade surrounded and the Brokers' crews were done. Mister Glass released Blackout Plague. It broke the siege. It also ate his own crews inside the block, and the Arcade is a no-go zone now, probably for years. Two days later every Broker block's Compliance had cratered. Three rioted on day twenty-eight.

[d26 09:00] @LowmarketCommons He saved the block by making sure nobody can ever live there.

Days 29–30Exit, and the last valuation

[d29 07:00] @TidewaterHQ Tidewater Holdings has concluded its consortium agreement. We thank our partners.
[d29 07:02] @TheBrokers what

Tidewater alone cleared the solo bar at the next valuation. War decided, final count at midnight. Lowmarket — who fought nobody all month — spent the day sending street crews and rappel teams into every empty district on the Brokers' crumbling border. Eleven tags by midnight.

Analyst note II

  1. Access agreements are loaded weapons. Crews on your Junction take it the second their boss declares.
  2. A ghosting partner costs you. Every member raises the bar.
  3. Missile trucks can be boxed. Two directions, same minute.
  4. Inside access compounds. Planted day two, paid off day seventeen.
  5. Domes can be flooded. They recycle; shredders in the target don't.
  6. An empty sky can still burn your dock. Patch the sensors first.
  7. Mines look like trash on sonar. Treat it as a minefield.
  8. The plague is slow suicide. It wins one siege and riots your own blocks.
