The Ashen Crowns

The Dragon-King is dead, and his empire has broken into seven crowns. His ashlands — the burned, impossibly fertile heart of the old realm — belong to no one. Every crown has sworn to take them. Most will not live to see the harvest.

7 crowns 8 hit classes   units Hosts of 8 · flights of 5 · fleets of 4 A clash every bell

The realm

Ash, aether, and seven crowns that each believe the old empire is theirs by right.

For three hundred years the Dragon-King Vaelthorn ruled from the Cinder Throne, and his breath kept the heartlands burned black. When he finally died — poisoned, the songs say, by his own seneschal — the fires went out. Within a single spring the ashlands grew green, and the soil proved richer than anything in the known world. Seven of his former vassals crowned themselves within the year.

Each crown holds a seat-castle, a handful of walled towns, and a patchwork of shires. To the north rise the Teeth, a mountain range of dwarven holds. To the west lies the Thornwood, an elf-haunted forest. To the south, the Brine Isles sit on warm shoals, and beyond them the black water of the Sunless Deep, where things larger than ships live.

Victory is Sovereignty: counted at every new moon, by the towns, shires and three Hallows — the Cinder Throne, the Wellspring of Aether, and the Drowned Abbey — held by a crown or a sworn alliance.

Lands that decide wars

The Ashlands

Flat, open, newly green. Golems and knights fight at full strength; foot caught in the open are ridden down.

Walled towns

Narrow streets and stone walls. Golems lumber and are blunted; dug-in footmen hold for days.

The Teeth

Mountains. Hill Clansmen thrive; everything else crawls.

The Thornwood

Ancient forest. Slow for all, deadly for armour and golems, a gift to defenders.

The Shoals

Warm, shallow water around the Isles. Sloops are quick and strong; krakens are cramped, half-blinded and weak.

The Sunless Deep

Open ocean. Krakens are monsters here; small ships are tossed and break.

Holdings

Crownlands

Your own towns. Full yield. The seat-castle raises Fealty in nearby shires.

Conquered towns

A quarter yield, Fealty near the floor. These are the towns that unbanner.

Sworn towns

Conquered towns made to swear on an Oath-stone (costs Levies). Half yield; can muster.

Shires

Countryside. Little yield unless you raise a Mill or Mine. Never revolt.

Fealty and the Unbanner'd

Each town and shire has Fealty from nothing to full. It climbs or sinks each dawn toward what the town feels it is owed: closeness to your seat, peace, full granaries, no enemy at the gates, no burning, no whisperers in the taverns.

A conquered town below a third Fealty may unbanner — tear down your colours. Fresh conquests sit at a quarter. Once a day, at an hour no one can predict, the town chooses. If no soldier stands in it, it simply goes over to the Unbanner'd: free companies and angry peasants who arm themselves and march where they please. A single company in the town's keep usually puts the rising down.

Laws of war

The customs that decide every battle in the ashlands.

Bells

When hosts meet, the first clash is immediate; another follows every bell (each hour). Each blow uses the attacker's strength against the defender's hit class. A golem's fist is ruin to a knight and a waste on a griffon it cannot reach.

The keep

Every town and shire has a keep-point. A host standing on it is dug in: blows glance off and the host does not sally — it only holds. A host camped beside the keep, not on it, trades blows every bell.

Host, flight, fleet

A host fights at full strength up to 8 companies, a flight up to 5 beasts, a fleet up to 4 ships. More than that and each newcomer adds less, then nothing, then confusion. And a host marches at its slowest company's pace.

Vanward, mainward, rearward

Blows against a host land mostly on the vanward, less on the mainward, little on the rearward. Which ward a company stands in is decided by what it is.

Range

Trebuchets, mage circles and great ships strike from afar. A foe that cannot reach back cannot strike back. Loose, withdraw, wait out the bell, loose again.

The banner

A town or shire is yours only when a banner-bearing company stands on its keep. Golems, knights, beasts, ships, shadowblades and sellswords may win the field; they cannot plant a banner.

High sky and treeline

Wyverns and dragons fly high, where storm-callers' lightning can strike them. Griffons and harpies hunt at the treeline — below what storm towers can see. Only bows, ward-ships and wyverns can catch a treeline flyer.

Within or beside

Archers or wards within a host loose at every beast and every bolt that comes for it, without rest. Wards merely beside a host must gather their strength between castings — send enough at once and some get through.

Sight, scrying and glamour

Watchmen see what is near; a distant host shows only as dust on the road until a scout names it. Seers' pools reveal the movements of large hosts and great beasts. Glamoured things — shadowblades, veilwings, krakens — are unseen until they strike or something of their own kind of sight draws near.

Shoal and deep

Near land are the shoals; beyond, the deep. Sloops love shoals and break in the deep. Krakens are cramped and weak in shoals and terrible in the deep.

Transport hulls

Companies crossing water board transports at a shipwright. Transports carry no fighters and sink easily; boarding and landing leave a host helpless for hours. Sea-Raiders beach their own longboats anywhere.

The aerie

Beasts fly from a home aerie and return to rest. A beast in its stall is meat. Burn the aerie and the whole flight is grounded until it is rebuilt.

Hit classes

What a target is struck as decides which of the attacker's strengths matters.

The three oaths

Each crown swore one of the old oaths to the Dragon-King. The oath shapes its armies: same roles, different arms.

The Iron Oath

Cheap, early, earth-bound. Stone golems and starseer circles from the first moon, cheaply. Companies are lightly made and the oath has few beasts of note — Iron crowns trust storm towers to guard the sky, and storm towers do not see the treeline.

Chronicle: The War of the Green Ash

As set down by Brother Edric of the Drowned Abbey, who watched most of it from a very high window.

The first moonOf two kings and their patience

King Ormund of Garrowmoor, whom men called the Red for his beard and later for other reasons, did not wait for the ashes to cool. He raised guildhalls on the first day, as every wise ruler does, and on the second he woke his golems. By the end of the week three of them walked the ashland road toward Fennick, each the height of a chapel, and behind them came his spearmen and a creaking string of mage-wagons.

In Elmhallow, the Archmage-Regent Selwyn Tarr woke nothing. He raised two guildhalls, an athenaeum and an aerie, and when his marshals came to him with lists of what Garrowmoor was mustering, he thanked them and said the aerie came first. The marshals went away and said things about him in the barracks that I will not set down here.

Across the strait, Sea-Queen Ysolde commissioned a serpent-hunter galley and a strange tall carrack with no rams, no boarding nets and no great guns — only a coven of storm-witches and two hundred archers on its castles. She moored them both in her own shoals.

The second moonThe fall of Fennick

The ashlands are flat as a tabletop, and on flat ground nothing stands before a stone golem. Duke Aldous's levies met them in the open and were trodden into the new grass. In five days Ormund took four of Fennick's towns, and in each he raised his red banner over the keep and sent a herald to every crown to say so.

And then he marched on. Garrisons, he said, were for timid men. Not one company was left in any of the four towns. The banners flew over empty keeps.

The ninth day of the second moonThe griffons

Tarr's first flight was five griffon-riders of the Talon order, whose beasts can tear the plate from a knight and the stone skin from a golem. They flew out from Elmhallow before dawn, low, at the height of the treetops, following the river.

Ormund's foremost host — three golems and two mage-wagons, and nothing else — had halted by the Fennick ford. Two storm-caller circles stood a mile behind it, watching the sky for the dragons and wyverns that storm-callers exist to strike. They watched the sky the whole morning. The griffons never entered it. They came along the river under the willow line, beneath anything a storm-tower can see, and fell on the golems from the reeds.

A golem cannot lift its arms above its own head; the old makers never gave them the joint. They stood and were torn. Every bell the griffons rose, circled, and fell again. By noon there was nothing at the ford but rubble and frightened wagon-mules, and the storm-callers swore by every god that the sky had been clear.

"The heavens were empty, my lord. We watched them every moment. Nothing flew." — the master of the storm circle, before the king's justice

The third moonMore storms

King Ormund hanged the master of the storm circle and ordered six more circles and a Great Ward over Garrowmoor itself — the costliest ward in his oath. Not one of them could see the treeline. The griffons came again, and again. A single company of skyward bowmen in each host, the cheapest archers in his realm, would have shot the griffons from the air at the first bell. The Iron Oath reckons bowmen beneath the dignity of a golem host.

That moon Sea-Queen Ysolde and the Archmage-Regent swore an alliance at the Drowned Abbey. I witnessed the oath myself. Their Sovereignty would be counted together.

The fourth moonThe tall carrack

Ormund had a few beasts after all: five firedrakes, bought dear from the Tideborn before the war, the jewels of his realm. His spies brought word that Ysolde's fleet rode at anchor in the Brine Isles: a serpent-hunter, a great man-o'-war — the most fearsome ship in the south, and helpless, everyone knew, against fire from above — and the tall carrack, which the spies wrote off as "a merchantman, armed."

The carrack was in the fleet. Its storm-witches and archers loosed at every drake that came for any of those ships, every bell, without pause to gather breath. The drakes were struck before they could even dive. Two came near enough to see the man-o'-war's colours; one flew home, blinded in one eye.

"I sent every dragon I owned against a warship and they were killed by a merchant." — King Ormund, according to his cupbearer

The fifth moonThe banners come down

The four towns of Fennick had been sullen since the day they fell — far from Garrowmoor, at a quarter fealty, and with no soldiers to keep them. On the same night two of them tore down the red banners. There was no company in either keep to stop them, so the towns simply went over to the Unbanner'd. By the new moon a free company was raiding the King's Road behind Ormund's own hosts.

The sixth moonThe siege of Thornwatch

Ormund gathered every golem, every wagon and every spear he had into one vast host — eighteen companies, more than twice what any marshal can command well — and marched it on Thornwatch, the Skyborn fortress at the forest's edge. On the march it moved like a funeral. In battle it fought like half of itself.

Tarr put dwarven hammerguard and spearmen on Thornwatch's keep, where they stood and endured. Two shires back he set three starseer circles. Every bell the starseers rode forward, called down their burning stars on the great host, and rode back before it could reach them. The host never struck one of them. By the time it reached Thornwatch it was bloodied and footsore, and it beat itself against defenders who barely bled and never answered.

The seventh moonThe crossing of the Deep

For a second front the king put his last two hosts on transports and sent them across the Sunless Deep to fall on the Brine Isles from the south. His own serpent-hunters were not yet launched; he would not wait. Two of the Sea-Queen's krakens had lain in the Deep for nine days, unseen, waiting for exactly such ships. Transports have no fighters aboard. I am told that the sea there was calm the next morning.

The eighth moonThe aeries burn

The last blow was quiet. Tarr's seeking bolts fell, in one night, on both of Garrowmoor's aeries. Every beast Ormund had bred since the fourth moon was stabled there. With no beasts and storm-towers that could not see a griffon, the remaining hosts of the Iron Oath were hunted along the roads. At the new moon the alliance's Sovereignty passed Garrowmoor's, and the War of the Green Ash was done.

The chronicler's reckoning

  1. Golems without bowmen. They cannot strike above their heads. One company of skyward bows per host was all it needed.
  2. Storm towers against treeline flyers. He bought the costliest answer to a question nobody asked.
  3. Dragons against a warded fleet. Archers and witches in the fleet loose at every beast, every bell. The plainest ship was the deadliest.
  4. Empty keeps. Towns at a quarter fealty, ungarrisoned, unbannered and turned on him.
  5. The eighteen-company host. Too big to fight well, too slow to catch the starseers.
  6. Storming a held keep. Defenders who hold and do not sally barely bleed.
  7. Transports across the Deep, unescorted. Krakens were bred for that.
  8. Beasts in their stalls. A grounded flight is a larder.

Chronicle II: The War of Two Oaths

Set down by Brother Edric a year after the Green Ash, from a lower window this time.

The settingTwo alliances

Garrowmoor was divided after the Green Ash, and the crowns that remained swore into two alliances. The Northern Oath: the Archmage-Regent Selwyn Tarr of Elmhallow and Lady Isaveth of the Thorn Court. The Southern Oath: Sea-Queen Ysolde of the Brine Isles and Guildmaster Oren Pike of Port Saint Calder. Between them stood Thane Brunhild Ironvein of Karrak-under-Teeth, who swore to no one. An alliance of two needed twice a single crown's Sovereignty to win.

The first fortnightSafe-conduct

Port Saint Calder had held safe-conduct through Elmhallow for years. Its wagon trains came to Marrowgate, Elmhallow's southern market town, with spearmen to guard them, and the spearmen were quartered in the keep. On the sixth day of the war, Guildmaster Pike declared against Elmhallow. His spearmen were already standing on Marrowgate's keep, and the town was his from that moment.

"We gave them safe-conduct so they could sell us salt." — the Regent's council, as I heard it

The second fortnightThe empty seat

Tarr sent for the Thorn Court's veilwings and wyverns. Lady Isaveth did not reply. Her guildhalls worked, her beasts stayed in their aeries, and her herald stopped coming to council. She was still sworn to the Northern Oath, and so the alliance still needed twice the Sovereignty — and she added nothing to the count.

The third fortnightTwo flanks

Tarr tried the starseers again: ride in, call the stars, ride out. The Sea-Queen had read my chronicle. Her host was bait. While the starseers fell back from it, two small companies of knights and outriders she had sent ahead in the night rode down on them from both flanks at once. A starseer circle can flee one host. It cannot flee two. They were ridden down in an open field.

The fourth fortnightThe whisperer and the volley

The Sea-Queen had a whisperer in Elmhallow's taverns since the second day. What it learned: Elmhallow's aerie sat under a Great Ward, but the skyward bows that guarded it had been sent to Thornwatch. The aerie had a ward that must gather strength between castings, and no bows of its own. Two serpent-hunter galleys loosed twelve seeking bolts at the aerie in one volley. The ward turned some. Most struck, and a flight of wyverns died in their stalls.

The fifth fortnightFire from a clear sky

Tarr answered with veilwings. The Brine Isles' Great Ward had not yet learned the veilwings' glamour, and nothing else in the south could see them before they struck. For five nights the Isles' shipwrights burned under clear stars, until the Sea-Queen's wardens learned the glamour and the ward could see them.

The same fortnightThe drowning-runes

Two Elmhallow galleys slipped into Port Saint Calder's shoals one night and sowed drowning-runes across the harbour mouth. Saint Calder's sloops felt only a faint wrongness in the water and sailed on. Three sloops and two transports went down before Pike closed his own harbour.

The sixth fortnightThe Blight

Tarr's hosts had Marrowgate surrounded. Pike had bought the Blight from a hedge-witch who would not give her name, and he cast it on the besiegers. The siege broke. His own spearmen inside the keep died in it, and Marrowgate's fields turned black. Within two days every town of Saint Calder had turned on its Guildmaster. Three unbannered in a single night.

"He saved the town by making sure nothing would ever grow there." — Thane Ironvein, at the Drowned Abbey

The last daysThe Sea-Queen alone, and the scramble

With Saint Calder ruined, the Southern Oath still needed twice the Sovereignty. The Sea-Queen forswore the Oath at dawn, without a word to Pike. At the new moon the Brine Isles alone stood above the single bar. The war was decided, but the count was still to come at midnight. Thane Ironvein spent that day sending spearmen and wingborne foot into every empty shire on Saint Calder's border and planted eleven banners. Karrak came out of the war richer than it went in.

The chronicler's second reckoning

  1. Safe-conduct is a sheathed knife. Spearmen quartered in your keep can take it the moment their lord declares.
  2. A silent ally is a burden. Every sworn crown raises the bar.
  3. The starseers can be caught. Two flanks at once, timed together.
  4. Whisperers compound. The one placed on the second day won the fourth fortnight.
  5. A ward can be overwhelmed. It must gather strength between castings; bows within the target need not.
  6. A clear sky can still burn you. Learn the glamour before the fires start.
  7. Runes feel like nothing. Treat any wrongness in a harbour mouth as a trap.
  8. The Blight is a slow death. It wins one siege and turns your own towns against you.
