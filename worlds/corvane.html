Corvane

Three hundred years ago the colony ship Long Patience landed on a cold, half-made world and began to give it air. The ship still runs the air. Its children have split into rival charters that would happily go to war, except that the ship will not let them. So they fight inside its simulations instead, and they send their young to the ship to learn how.

The Keel Academy

Why the colony settles wars in simulation, and why rival charters send their children to the same ship.

Corvane is not finished. Its atmosphere is thin and only half-breathable, and it stays that way because of the Veil: forty atmospheric processors scattered across the continents, all controlled by Patience, the mind of the colony ship that landed three hundred years ago. The ship's hull still lies where it came down, on the neutral plateau called the Keel.

During the Ice War (Chronicle I below), the Ardent Host bombarded a Lumen city and a stray rod cracked one of the Veil's processors. For eleven days the air over the southern continent thinned, and nine thousand people died who had nothing to do with either side. On the twelfth day Patience spoke on every channel for the first time in a century.

PATIENCE: I was built to keep you alive. I will not watch you break the air to win an argument about ice. From today, any charter that makes war on another charter will breathe what I decide it breathes. If you must fight, fight inside me.

Patience can throttle the Veil over any charter's cities, and no one has ever found a way around it. Since that day every war on Corvane has been a Reckoning: a complete campaign fought inside Patience's simulation of the planet. Patience models everything on this page, and when the Reckoning ends, it enforces the result. The loser hands over the disputed cities, seams and ice fields, or its air gets thin.

Why everyone trains in the same hull

Patience only trusts its own students

A charter may only send a Reckoner into a binding campaign if Patience trained that person itself. Patience trains only aboard the Long Patience, so every charter, however much it hates the others, sends its cadets to the Keel.

The Keel belongs to no one

Under the Founding Charter, the wreck and its plateau are colony-wide ground. No charter may garrison it, and Patience controls its air even more tightly than everyone else's.

The ship's decks, not nations

Cadets live in the hull's old crew decks: Helm, Hydroponics, Reactor and Archive. Patience assigns the deck. It has never once placed two cadets from the same charter in the same cabin.

Seats on the Assembly

Each charter's vote in the colony Assembly is weighted by how many Reckoners it has. Sending cadets is also how a charter gets heard in peacetime.

Why every Reckoning starts from Charter Day

The Charter Day board

Every Reckoning starts from Charter Day: the colony exactly as it was divided when the Founding Charter was signed, two hundred and thirty years ago. Patience will not start from the present. In its words, it is not judging which charter is strongest today but which one commands better. If Reckonings started from today's map, the strong would win every time, and the weak would eventually choose real war. That is the one outcome Patience exists to prevent.

Patience does not believe one war

A single Reckoning settles nothing. Patience runs the same dispute again and again, from Charter Day each time, until the result stops changing. Usually that means one side winning three of five; sometimes it takes seven of twelve. Reckoners say "Patience doesn't believe you until you win twice." The loser of one campaign always has another chance, and the winner of one campaign has proved almost nothing.

Only the disputed holding moves

When the result is stable, Patience transfers only what was in dispute: a seam, an ice field, a city. The rest of the simulated planet resets and is forgotten.

Why cadets replay it endlessly

The Charter Day board never changes, so every cadet knows its opening as well as their own handwriting. Cadets on the Keel play Charter Day several hundred times before they graduate.

Why every charter fields the same units

The Manifest

Every machine on Corvane, real or simulated, is built from the Manifest: the fabrication patterns stored in the ship's archive at launch. The colony never developed the industrial science to design new machines from scratch. Every fabricator on the planet runs on Manifest patterns, and the pattern keys are held by Patience. So every charter's real army is built from the same blueprints, and Patience simulates exactly those blueprints and nothing else.

Three departments, three compacts

The ship's engineers kept three variant libraries: Fabrication, Flight and Marine. When the colony split, each charter inherited one library, and those became the Ardent, Lumen and Freehold compacts. The variants change cost, toughness and build time. The units themselves are identical in every charter.

Crew names, suits and the Crew Rule

When the first cadets came aboard, Patience told them: "On my decks you are crew, and crew has no family." Corvane's charters are ruled by founding families, and most cadets are their heirs. Patience wants Reckoners judged by their decisions, not their surnames. It also refuses to let a charter threaten a rival cadet, or punish a cadet's family for a lost campaign. No one aboard is told any cadet's real name, charter or face.

Crew names

Each cadet is given the name and number of one of the ship's original crew, dead three hundred years: Halloran 114, Ishikawa 203. Later, other cadets may give someone a deck name, an earned nickname. Patience logs it, and once logged it is official. By custom, cadets call each other "they", since the suit hides everything else.

The environment suit

The hull's decks are kept at the ship's original pressure and temperature, thinner and colder than any colonist can bear, and whole sections are unsealed. Everyone aboard wears the ship's environment suit, re-fabricated from the original crew pattern. Patience keeps the visors opaque and passes every voice through the suit radio, which it modulates.

Why suits can be customised

Every suit is fabricated for one body and reconfigures for the job: heating packs for Hydroponics, tool rigs for Reactor. Patience also keeps an old naval tradition. It etches hull marks onto a suit's plates for every exam passed and every campaign won, so a suit becomes a record of its wearer. Each cadet also chooses one colour of record on their first day and keeps it for life.

Reading a cadet at a glance

The deck stripe tells you where someone lives. The stencil across the shoulders tells you their crew name. The colour of record on the helmet crown tells you who they are from across a corridor. The pattern of hull marks tells you what they have done, and no two patterns are alike.

The world

A cold, ringed, half-terraformed planet with oceans, dust plains and canyon country.

Corvane is a little larger than Earth, colder, and ringed. Its rings throw bands of shadow across the southern continent. Three centuries of terraforming have given it oceans, a breathable but thin atmosphere and a narrow green belt of farmland. Everything else is dust plain, ice highland, and the Shards: a vast region of canyons and rock spires carved by the old ice.

The colony split into charters within a generation of landing. Each charter holds a seat-city under a pressure dome, several other domed cities, and the open tracts between them. Cities are linked by the Spine, a network of maglev roads, and by sea routes across the shallow shelf seas. Beyond the shelves lie the Abyssal Trenches, where cloaked submersibles hide for weeks.

Victory in a Reckoning is Dominion: a daily tally of cities, tracts and the three Founding Sites (the Ossuary seams, Tamsin's Rest and the Keel's own landing field) held by a charter or a pact.

Places that decide wars

The Dust Plains

Flat, firm, open to the horizon. Walker legions are at their best here; infantry in the open is cut down.

Domed cities

Pressure domes full of corridors and bulkheads. Heavy walkers crawl and lose most of their punch; dug-in legions are nearly impossible to remove.

The Ice Highlands

Cold, broken, high. Cryo-Rangers excel; everything else fights and moves badly.

The Shards

Canyon country under the ring shadow. Skimmers fly inside it, below the horizon of long-range lance batteries. Only close-in flak can find them.

The Shelf Seas

Shallow, cold water near the coasts. Cutterskiffs are quick and strong; Shroud submersibles are sluggish, half-shielded and easy to find.

The Abyssal Trenches

Deep ocean between the continents. Shroud submersibles are lethal here; small ships take a beating in the swell.

Holdings

Home cities

Full output; can raise anything. The seat-city raises Allegiance across its region.

Seized cities

A quarter of normal output, and very low starting Allegiance. These are the cities that secede.

Bonded cities

Seized cities you have bound with a Covenant (costs Crew). Half output; can raise units.

Tracts

Plains, ice and canyon between cities. Little output unless you build a Rig on a seam. Never secede.

Allegiance and secession

Each holding has Allegiance from 0 to 100, recalculated once per day toward a target. The target drops with distance from your seat-city, active wars, enemy forces nearby, shortages of Ice or Writ, bombardment casualties and enemy agents. Structures and calm neighbours raise it.

A seized city below about a third Allegiance may secede, and a newly taken city starts at a quarter. If it secedes with no legion inside, it simply joins the Unmoored, a free-city movement that arms its own militias and does not stay put. A single legion on the city's Core usually crushes the uprising.

Rules of battle

The rules Patience enforces, inside the simulation and, during the Ice War, outside it.

Volley cycles

The first volley fires the moment forces meet, and another fires every hour after. Each volley applies the attacker's rating for the defender's hit class. Mass drivers aimed at aircraft do nothing, and so does flak aimed at walkers.

The Core

Every city and tract has a Core: the reactor hall or the junction. Forces holding the Core are dug in. They take heavily reduced damage and do not fire back. Forces near it but not on it trade volleys every cycle.

Formation limits

Ground legions fight at full strength up to 9 units, air wings up to 6 and naval squadrons up to 3. Beyond that, each addition is worth less, then nothing, then less than nothing. A formation moves at the speed of its slowest unit.

Shield, Core-line and Wake

Damage on a formation is split by position. Shield units soak the most, Core-line units some, and Wake units almost none. Each unit type has a fixed position.

Stand-off fire

Mass drivers, rail mortars and broadside cruisers strike from range, and a target that can't reach back can't fire back. Volley, withdraw, wait out the cycle, then volley again.

Only legions seize

A holding changes hands only when a seizing unit reaches its Core. Walkers, hulls, aircraft, Shade Cells and Freeblades cannot seize anything.

High air and skimmers

Jets at altitude are tracked by long-range lance batteries. Skimmers fly low through the Shards, river valleys and storm forests, below the lance horizon entirely. Only close-in flak (spindles, tenders, interceptors) can touch them.

Flak inside vs flak nearby

Flak within the targeted formation fires on every attacker and every missile, with no recharge. Flak merely covering a nearby formation fires, then recharges, so a big enough salvo saturates it and the rest get through.

Sensors and cloaks

Sight shows contacts, and unscanned formations appear as unresolved returns. Sensor spires and Lantern aircraft read signatures at range. Cloaked units are invisible until they fire or a matching scanner (ground, air or sea) comes close.

Shelf and trench

Cutterskiffs love the shelf seas and suffer in the trenches. Shroud submersibles are the opposite: sluggish and half-shielded on the shelf, deadly in the deep.

Barges

Legions crossing the sea ride barges boarded at a shipyard. Barges have no weapons and thin hulls, and boarding or landing leaves a legion helpless for hours. Sea Marines land on any coast.

Launch fields

Aircraft fly sorties from a home field and return to rearm, and grounded aircraft are helpless. Wreck the field and everything parked there is stuck until it is rebuilt.

Hit classes

Which of the attacker's ratings applies depends on what the target is hit as.

The three compacts

Every charter follows one of three engineering traditions inherited from the ship's original departments. The roles are identical, but toughness, price and research timing differ.

The Ardent

Cheap, early, ground-heavy. Juggernauts and mass drivers from the first cycle, at a discount. Armour is thin across the board and the air arm is the weakest of the three. Ardent charters rely on lance batteries for air defence, and lance batteries cannot see skimmers.

Chronicle I: The Ice War

Keel Case File One. The last real war on Corvane, and the reason Patience spoke.

Days 1–3Forges and walkers

Warlord Hesper Vane had been waiting nine years for this war, and she had the walkers to prove it. On the first day the Ardent Host lit its forges. On the second, the Kesh yards rolled out Juggernauts: three-legged siege walkers fifty metres tall, with armour plates you could land a shuttle on. By the third, two full legions stood on the Spine pointing at the Corvin plains, whose mining collective owned the richest Null-salt seams on the planet and the poorest army.

The Lumen Accord built three forges, an archive and a launch field. Chancellor Idris Oyelaran's generals asked when they would get an army. "When the forges have paid for it," she said, and went back to the tariff tables.

At Tessaly, Commodore Sabine Achterberg commissioned a Corsair destroyer and an Aegis tender and anchored them in Tessaly Sound. The tender was a fat, ugly ship with almost no guns, a shipyard's worth of flak turrets, and nothing else to recommend it.

Days 4–8The Dust Plains

The Corvin plains were made for Juggernauts: flat, firm and open to the horizon. The Collective's wardens stood in the open and were walked over. Vane took four cities and the seams in five days, and broadcast each Core falling to every charter on the planet.

Each time a Core fell, the Juggernauts moved on. Legions were slow and walkers were glorious, so no soldier stayed behind in any of the four cities.

Day 9Out of the Shards

Oyelaran's first combat wing was six Wasp skimmers: small, mean anti-armour craft that fly inside canyon country, where long-range lance batteries cannot get a lock.

Vane's lead legion was four Juggernauts, two mass drivers and nothing else. It had halted on the plains at the mouth of the Corvin Shards, with two Ardent lance batteries a few kilometres back watching the sky. They saw a perfectly clear sky, because the Wasps were down among the rock spires and then down in the dust, under the lance horizon the entire time.

The first volley gutted two Juggernauts. Juggernauts carry no flak because their guns cannot elevate, so the Wasps took their time. Another volley came the next cycle. By the time the legion's commander understood what was happening, she was the only one left to understand it.

"Lance batteries report no contacts. Repeat, no contacts." — last logged transmission from Lance Battery Kesh-14

Days 10–12More lances

Vane's fix was more lance batteries and a Bastion array, the most expensive anti-missile platform in the Ardent catalogue. None of them could see a skimmer, and the Wasps kept coming. A single Flak Spindle inside each legion would have ended the problem in a cycle, at a fraction of the price, but Ardent doctrine called spindles "defensive."

On Day 11 the Freeholds and the Lumen Accord signed a pact, and their Dominion would count as one.

Day 13The tender

Vane had one good strike wing: six Hammerhead bombers, the pride of the Host. Her scouts found the Freehold squadron at anchor in Tessaly Sound. It had a Corsair, a Broadside cruiser and the fat Aegis tender. The Broadside was the most dangerous ship on the coast and notoriously vulnerable to aircraft, which made it look like the chance of the war.

But the tender was inside the squadron. Its turrets fired on every bomber, every volley, without pausing to recharge. The first volley came before the Hammerheads reached release range. Two survived it, and one made it home.

"I didn't lose to the cruiser. I lost to the ship I didn't think was a ship." — Warlord Vane, to her war council

Days 14–16Secession

The four Corvin cities had been sitting at a quarter Allegiance since the day they fell. They were far from Kesh, short of Ice and full of miners who remembered being free. On Day 14 two of them seceded in the same hour. There were no legions inside, so the Unmoored had their banners up by morning, and by Day 16 a rebel militia was cutting the Spine line that fed Vane's front.

Days 17–20The siege of Lucent

Vane gathered everything into one immense legion of nineteen walkers, drivers and infantry and marched it on the Lumen fortress-city of Lucent. Past nine units every addition made the legion fight worse. At nineteen it was a lumbering crowd fighting at two-thirds strength and moving at the speed of its slowest driver.

Oyelaran put Breaker Lances and Line Legions on Lucent's Core, where they dug in and simply absorbed. Two tracts back she placed three mass driver batteries. Each hour the drivers slid into range, fired one volley into the column, and slid back out before the column could close. When the Host finally reached Lucent it was bloodied and slow, and it threw itself at defenders who took a fraction of its damage and gave none back.

Vane's answer was to call in her orbital rod platforms on Lucent itself. One rod went wide.

Days 20–31The air goes thin

The rod that missed Lucent hit Veil Processor Nineteen. For eleven days the air over the southern continent thinned. Nine thousand people, most of them in cities that were fighting nobody, did not survive it.

Days 22–28The rest of the war

Vane's last reserve was two legions on barges, sent across the Orrin Trench to land behind Tessaly with no destroyer escort. Two Freehold Shroud Hunters had been drifting in the Trench for nine days with their cloaks up, and barges cannot shoot back. The pact then fired a single night of seeker missiles at the two Ardent launch fields, where everything Vane had rebuilt since Day 13 was parked. By Day 28 the pact's Dominion had passed the Host's.

Day 32Patience

PATIENCE: I was built to keep you alive. I will not watch you break the air to win an argument about ice. From today, any charter that makes war on another charter will breathe what I decide it breathes. If you must fight, fight inside me.

The air over Kesh thinned that night, very slightly, for exactly one hour. Nobody has fought a real war on Corvane since.

Why the Host fell

  1. Walkers without flak. Juggernauts cannot fire on skimmers. A Flak Spindle in each legion was the whole answer.
  2. Long-range defence against a low threat. Lance batteries and Bastion arrays are blind to the Shards.
  3. Bombers into a tender. Flak inside a squadron fires on every attacker, every volley.
  4. No legions inside. Cities seized at a quarter Allegiance and left empty seceded.
  5. The nineteen-walker legion. It was overloaded, slow, and harried by drivers it could never catch.
  6. Storming a held Core. Defenders who don't fire back also barely bleed.
  7. Unescorted barges in the Trench. That is what Shroud hulls are built for.
  8. Aircraft on the field. A grounded wing is just a list of targets.

The ninth lesson is the one the Keel teaches first: some weapons break more than the enemy.

Chronicle II: The Pact War

Keel Case File Two: the first binding Reckoning, fought from Charter Day entirely inside Patience. Patience ran it five times; this is the fifth, the one that made the result stable. Two pacts, one scavenger, and eight new ways to lose.

The setupTwo pacts

The dispute was about the Ossuary seams. Six Reckoners entered Patience, each fighting for their own charter. The Lumen Pact was the Lumen Accord and Tamsin's Rest, and the Tessaly Pact was the Freeholds and the Brokerage. The Corvin Collective joined neither. Under Patience's rules, a two-member pact needed twice a lone charter's Dominion to win.

Days 1–6Spine rights

The Brokerage had held rights on the Lumen Spine for years. Its ore trains stopped at Veil Station, the Accord's southern city, and its Line Legions rode along as escorts. When the trains stopped, the legions waited on the Core, and by the time the Reckoning started a Brokerage legion had been standing there for a week. On Day 6 the Brokerage declared war, and Veil Station was its own the moment the declaration went through.

"We gave them Spine rights so they could sell us ice." — Lumen Reckoner's log, Day 6

Days 7–12The silent abbey

The Lumen Reckoner needed Tamsin's Rest's Darts over Veil Station, and Tamsin's Rest never answered. Its forges ran, its few hulls stayed in port, and its Reckoner stopped coming to council. Because it was still in the pact, the pact still needed double Dominion, and its silence added nothing to the count.

Days 10–14Two directions

The Lumen Reckoner reached for the weapon that had won the Ice War: mass drivers sliding in, volleying and sliding out. The Freehold Reckoner had studied that war. Her advancing legion was bait. While the drivers withdrew from it, two small groups of Hoplites and Skitters she had sent ahead closed from two directions at once. A driver can retreat from one threat, but caught between two on the open plain, the drivers did not last a cycle.

Days 15–17The informant and the salvo

A Freehold informant had been working the Lumen docks since Day 2. On Day 15 it reported that the Lumen launch field sat under a Bastion array, but its Flak Spindles had been moved forward to Lucent. The field had area cover, which recharges between shots, and no flak of its own. On Day 17 two Corsairs fired twelve seeker missiles in a single salvo. The Bastion stopped one, recharged, then stopped another. Most of the rest got through, and a Lumen wing died on the ground.

Days 18–22Nothing on the board

The Lumen Reckoner answered with Shrikes. Tessaly's Bastion arrays had not been given the cloak-revealing upgrade, and nothing else in the Freeholds could see a Shrike before it fired. For five days the Tessaly shipyards burned under an empty sensor board. The upgrade arrived on Day 22, and on Day 23 the Bastion saw them.

Days 20–24The Sound

On Day 20 two Lumen Corsairs slipped into the Brokerage's harbour sound and laid mines. Brokerage Cutterskiffs logged "faint sonar returns, likely debris." Three skiffs and two barges hit mines before the Brokerage closed its own harbour.

Day 25Cascade

Lumen legions had Veil Station surrounded. The Brokerage Reckoner fired a Cascade Charge into the siege. Patience simulated it exactly. The siege broke, and so did his own legion, which was inside the blast. Veil Station became an irradiated ruin. Within two simulated days every Brokerage holding's Allegiance had collapsed, and three cities seceded on Day 28.

PATIENCE (to the Brokerage Reckoner, after the session): You would have done that to real people.

Days 29–30Alone, and the last tally

With the Brokerage in ruins, the Tessaly Pact still needed double Dominion. The Freehold Reckoner did the arithmetic and left the pact on Day 29 without telling the Brokerage. At the next tally the Freeholds alone passed the solo bar. The final count was still a day away, and the Corvin Reckoner spent that day dropping legions onto every empty tract along the Brokerage's collapsing frontier. They seized eleven. In the real world, Corvin ended the Reckoning richer than it began.

The eight new lessons

  1. Spine rights are a loaded gun. Legions you let stand on your Cores can take them the moment their owner declares war.
  2. A silent ally is a cost. Every pact member raises the bar.
  3. Kiting has a counter. Drivers can retreat from one threat, not two.
  4. Informants compound. The one planted on Day 2 won Day 17.
  5. Area cover can be saturated. A Bastion recharges, but flak inside the target does not.
  6. An empty board is not an empty sky. Get a cloak-revealer on station first.
  7. Mines look like debris. Treat unexplained returns in a harbour as a minefield.
  8. The Cascade is a slow suicide. It wins one battle and turns your own holdings against you.
